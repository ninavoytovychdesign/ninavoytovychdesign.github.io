Context

Every day a mom has to keep several kids’ medications and appointments in her head – and at the doctor’s, find the right test result in a minute and hand it over.

The goal: one app where the daily plan and the medical history live side by side without mixing. Constraints: I work solo, with no client, and the data is medical and about children.

65% had never used medical apps before, so simplicity matters as much as features: 68% chose it, 57% chose security.

A survey of 37 people showed what to store and what was missing, 6 in-depth interviews gave me personas and a CJM, and usability tests of the first version with five participants showed where people got stuck. Most got through navigation unaided – what had to change was sign-in, health record creation and tone.

Personas and CJM

The interviews produced two personas, each with a CJM from need to outcome. Anna is the primary one: the MVP is built around her. Igor’s needs – Apple Health integration and analytics – I moved to after the MVP.

Persona

Anna, 26 – mom of two

Goals
All the kids’ medical records in one place, reminders about vaccines and medication, less time spent looking for documents
Pains from the CJM
Chaotic records and no quick way to find things, the risk of missing important events, lots of data to enter at sign-up and input errors, fear of losing data
Persona

Igor, 37 – works in IT, an active user

Goals
One place for all his data, integration with Google Health and Apple Health, automatic reminders about tests
Pains from the CJM
Scattered data, entering parameters by hand, a complicated integration and sync setup, no analytics – just a list of records

Structure

Three tabs for everyday use – Home, Records, Calendar – and one “+” for any entry. The MVP has health records, the daily plan, documents and the letter to the doctor. Shared access and Numa AI are deliberately postponed.

Decisions
Decision 01

Home shows the day, not a menu

Why
The core scenario from the interviews: every day a mom juggles several kids’ medications, her own reminders and appointments. In tests of the first version, participants asked for a helper character and a warmer tone.
What I did
A plan on a day timeline with a “now” marker; missed items are highlighted. Tasks without a time go into a separate “Keep an eye on” block.
Result
A droplet mascot now voices the state of the day: “I don’t see a check-in at 08:00”. Home answers “what now” instead of pointing to sections.

StatusMascot added after tests of the first version. The day plan is in the MVP prototype.

Decision 02

One “+” for 11 entry types

Why
Entries belong to different people. An “add” button in every section would make people first look for where the entry belongs.
What I did
“What to add?” → “For whom” → form. People think about the event, not about where to store it.
Result
All 11 types are added through one path – no separate button in every record and section.

StatusThe decision is in the MVP prototype. In the first version’s tests most people first looked for “create” at the top, so the “+” stayed for everyday entries, and the first record got its own explicit entry point: in onboarding and in the row of records.

Decision 03

No password, consent as its own step

Why
In the first version’s tests a wrong password was part of the scenario: participants got through recovery without trouble, but reached for the more visible Google sign-in, and some wanted to reset the password straight away. And the data is about children and health – consent can’t be small print.
What I did
Apple, Google or an e-mail code. Consent and 18+ confirmation before the first health record.
Result
The first record starts with three fields – name, date of birth, sex. The rest is added gradually.

StatusPassword removed after tests.

Decision 04

A health record you can hand to the doctor

Why
What matters differs for each child, and at an appointment it has to be passed on fast, sometimes in another language.
What I did
Overview sections are customizable, export builds a PDF, and the letter to the doctor fills in contacts and helps with translation.
Result
The record became a document for the appointment, not just an archive: PDF and a letter to the doctor from one screen.

StatusPart of the MVP scope.

System

States and system

A third of the screens are off the happy path: empty states, errors with explanations, confirmation of irreversible actions, system notifications. Everything is built from the UI kit: 34 components, 4 variable collections, 19 text styles. Each person’s colour is a supporting cue in the records and the calendar, but not the only way to tell people apart. A prototype limitation: in the new-account scenario it kept only one added record – critical for families with several children, so multiple records need checking before the next round of tests.
