Context

The app already existed, but the design and the branding had aged, and new services had no place to go.

So it was a redesign, not a facelift: a new system, a new home screen, sign-in and transport, plus services the app never had. We worked on the app for 10 months; the first MVP in the new design went live in a month and a half.

Constraints: releases alongside development, no research with people, and text size in the accessibility modes is calculated by developers with a formula.

Role

My role

We ran flows in parallel and reviewed each other’s work; the brand book, icons and animations are not mine. Fully mine: the UI kit and styles, accessibility, sign-in, the Invincibility map, and the redesign of news, surveys and the card application. We took the first versions of maps and transport through together.

Accessibility

Accessibility and system

Accessibility is part of the system, not a feature at the end: high contrast, greyscale and text at 100, 130 and 160%, with cards growing with the text. Underneath are 48 semantic tokens in four modes, so a theme or contrast mode is a mode switch, not a new set of mockups. Buttons are 48 px: the client asked for narrower ones and accepted 48 after I argued with the standards.

Decisions
Decision 01

The home screen adapts to the person

Why
People open the app signed out, without a Kharkiv resident card, with a card, or with an application under review or rejected. The old home showed the same tiles to everyone.
What I did
Home changes with the state: news and “Sign in with ID.GOV.UA”, news for people without a card, the X-card and quick actions for people with one.
Result
Everyone sees their own next step instead of the same set of tiles.
Decision 02

A card status always has a next step

Why
In the old version a status was text on a banner. A blocked card said “call the hotline”, and there was nothing else to do in the app.
What I did
A screen for each status: what happened and what to do. Application rejected – “Fix the application”; card blocked – “Contact support”. A placeholder for the card while the application is under review.
Result
The rejection and block screens carry the next action – right there, with no extra steps.
Decision 03

Sign-in: the same methods, a clearer choice and every state

Why
The old sign-in opened with a paragraph about BankID, with the other methods as identical rows below.
What I did
Sign-in through ID.GOV.UA with grouped methods: e-signature (file, token, cloud storage), Bank ID NBU, Diia.Signature, Diia.OAuth. A “Me” or “My child” choice, date of birth, secure sign-in with a code and biometrics.
Result
17 new states that weren’t there before: expired code, too many attempts, no internet, sign-in cancelled, passport not found, session ended and more.
Decision 04

Transport and metro

Why
Names and CTAs were unclear, search and timetables needed rework, and end stations had no scenarios of their own, and the app had no metro at all.
What I did
New names and CTAs, search, timetable structure, end-station scenarios, a new metro section with lines, timetables and exits, a limit on saved routes. Separate states: air-raid alert on the map, offline, location off.
Result
The metro shows directions, weekday and weekend timetables and open exits; the map shows accessible transport.

StatusThe flows were approved by the client. The timetable was reworked several times at their request; no testing with people.

New

New, not there before

The old version had none of these services. The biggest of mine are requests to a deputy and the “For Defenders” booking.

“My deputy”: requests with clear statuses

People sent a request and didn’t know what was happening with it or where to find replies. Now there are statuses, a reason if a request isn’t reviewed, and the whole reply history in the request card, with documents and a rating.

“For Defenders”: booking a visit from the phone

You pick a request category, read the service description, choose a date and a free time slot and fill in the applicant’s details. After booking there’s a ticket with the details, and the booking appears in the list on the service home. Institutions can be found on a map or in a list, filtered by district and type. Separate states: no free time on the date, booking failed, booking cancelled by the institution, missed visit.

Filters on one screen, text you can read

Each event filter used to open as a separate screen. Now all filters are on one screen, and selected ones clear with one tap. Headings 26 px, body 16 px, buttons 48 px.

Outcome
  1. 01

    A city app holds together through shared rules: I turned 33 colours from the approved screens into styles and merged the toasts into one component, and news, transport and requests began to behave the same way.

  2. 02

    Not everything was accepted: I proposed surveying people about what Home needs and making it lighter, but Home stayed as approved.

  3. 03

    Without research, approval didn’t answer whether things got easier. Next I’d test transport search, request statuses and the enlarged-text interface with people.
