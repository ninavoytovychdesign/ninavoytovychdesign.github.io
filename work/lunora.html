Context

Lunora is my own project where I went through the full cycle: a survey, desk research, a hypothesis, scenarios, states, a UI kit, four usability tests of the prototype and a metrics plan. The focus is on what study projects usually skip: errors, timeouts and retries.

Research

What the research showed

An analysis of banking and subscription managers showed the same: processing is often “empty”, and an error with no next step erodes trust. Hence the hypothesis: explaining the status, the reason and the next action gives people more sense of control.

Survey · 9 people · key findings
6 of 9

Have seen a charge for a subscription they forgot about

8 of 9 use two or more cards or accounts
9 of 9

Four of them – if the interface is simple
7 of 9

No one is negative. 6 would trust it more if features could be turned off

4 of 9 name stress as the hardest part of managing money

5 of 9

Want to avoid unexpected charges

9 of 9 manage money through banking apps

The sample is small and homogeneous: all nine are employed, eight are aged 21–40. It gives a direction for decisions, not a test of the hypothesis.

Decisions
Decision 01

Processing explains what’s happening

Why
Two of the four participants in the prototype test said that payment processing took too long. That made me look not only at the waiting time but at how the interface tells people what state the operation is in: when you don’t understand what’s happening, the wait feels longer and creates uncertainty.
What I did
Before, the steps “Checking card → Confirming payment → Almost done” were just text. I added a progress ring and checkmarks on completed steps, and highlighted the next step. There are no percentages: the duration depends on external payment systems.
Result
You can see which step the payment is on and that it’s moving forward, not stuck.

StatusThe changes are in the prototype. Retesting hasn’t yet confirmed how well they work.

Decision 02

Timeout isn’t Failed

Why
During a timeout, people don’t know whether the money was charged and aren’t sure whether to repeat the payment. If the timeout is shown as an error, they may pay twice.
What I did
Two different states. Failed – a no-blame message and two actions: Retry or Change payment method. Timeout – a neutral “We’re still processing the payment”.
Result
A timeout no longer looks like an error, so there’s no reason to pay twice.

StatusA shared pattern in the UI kit with the same CTA logic.

Decision 03

A next step in every state

Why
When a payment fails, people don’t see a clear next step and don’t know when the subscription will be active again.
What I did
Subscription details show the status, amount, next payment date and method. After success – a short confirmation and a return to Active with the new date.
Result
After any payment outcome, people know whether the subscription is active and when the next payment is due.

StatusIn the prototype with Smart Animate.

Decision 04

Onboarding and wallets without pressure

Why
Finance apps often add pressure, and 4 of 9 respondents name stress as the hardest part of managing money.
What I did
Onboarding asks what the person wants to track, the notification request explains the benefit, and a wallet can be connected or skipped.
Result
People choose what to connect and when, with no mandatory steps.

StatusIn the prototype.

AI is a helper, not the source of truth

AI is meant as a contextual helper: a short explanation of the current situation and the available actions in a bottom sheet. It doesn’t decide on its own whether a charge went through: statuses have to come from reliable payment infrastructure, and the concept has no such integration.

Progress indicators

In Lunora I separated two kinds of processes: the initial product setup and the processing of financial transactions.

Wallet setup – determinate progress
The Setting things up screen uses a 0–100% indicator, which in the prototype shows the expected interface behaviour during setup. In the real product this indicator has to be tied to the actual completion of the steps. If the backend can’t provide reliable progress data, an indicator without percentages is planned.
Payment processing – indeterminate progress
While a payment is being processed, the duration depends on external payment systems. A percentage indicator could therefore create a false expectation of when it will finish. For this scenario I chose a status interface that explains the current step, without artificial percentages.

The principle: a progress indicator has to show reliable information, not create an illusion of control.

Accessibility is built into the system: high contrast in the dark UI, a clear type hierarchy, large tap targets and short “what happened → what to do” messages. There haven’t been tests with assistive technologies yet – next come a WCAG review and screen reader flows.
